Hand rankings

Best to worst · your hand is your best five cards

1

Royal flush

A♥K♥Q♥J♥T♥

A, K, Q, J, 10 — all the same suit. The best possible hand. You may never see one; that's fine.

2

Straight flush

8♠7♠6♠5♠4♠

Five in a row, all the same suit. Rarer than everything below it.

3

Four of a kind

Q♠Q♥Q♦Q♣3♠

All four of the same rank — "quads." Here, four queens.

4

Full house

K♠K♥K♣7♠7♦

Three of a kind plus a pair. Here, three kings and two 7s — said as "kings full of sevens."

5

Flush

A♦J♦8♦5♦2♦

Five cards all the same suit, in any order. Beaten only by the four hands above.

6

Straight

9♠8♥7♣6♦5♠

Five cards in a row, any suits. Ace can be high (10-J-Q-K-A) or low (A-2-3-4-5).

7

Three of a kind

9♠9♥9♦K♠4♥

Three of the same rank — "trips" or a "set." Here, three 9s.

8

Two pair

J♠J♦5♠5♥A♠

Two different pairs. Here, two jacks and two 5s.

9

One pair

T♠T♥A♠7♦3♠

Two cards of the same rank. Here, a pair of 10s. Very common.

10

High card

A♥Q♠9♦5♣2♠

Nothing connects — no pair, no straight, no flush. Your best single card plays. Here, "ace high."

The pattern: rarer hands win. A flush is harder to make than a straight, so it beats it. Memorize the order and you've got the one thing you truly must know cold.

Ties broken by the "kicker": if two players both have one pair of 10s, the highest side card decides it. A-kicker beats K-kicker, and so on.

Suits never break ties. No suit beats another — a spade flush and a heart flush of equal cards split the pot.

Foundations. Same rankings in every form of Hold'em — online, live, home game. Standard NLHE.
