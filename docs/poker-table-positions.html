Button (BTN)

Dealt order is not acting order

The single thing beginners mix up. Cards are dealt starting left of the button (SB first) and ending on the button — that's literally why it holds the dealer button. But who acts first is different:

Preflop: action starts at UTG; the blinds act last (BB has "the option" to raise). The blinds wait because their money's already in.

Postflop: it flips — blinds act first, button acts last on every street.

The only number that matters for strategy is who acts last, and that's the button. Acting last means you've seen everyone else decide before you do. That's the whole reason the button opens ~45% of hands and UTG opens ~15%. Position isn't a seat — it's information.

In position vs out of position

You saw "in position" and "out of position" on the seat cards above. Here's what they mean, because these two phrases run through everything in poker.

First, one word to define: a street is just one round of betting. After the first two cards are dealt, there are three more betting rounds — the flop, the turn, and the river. Each of those is a "street," and on each one the players take turns acting in a set order.

In position (IP): you act after your opponent on every street after the flop.

Out of position (OOP): you act before your opponent.

That's the entire definition — it's just who has to move first. And it comes straight from where you sit relative to the button, which is why "position" and "who acts last" are really the same idea.

Why it matters more than almost anything else: acting last is a permanent information edge. When you're in position, you get to watch what your opponent does — bet, check, how big — before you have to decide anything. When you're out of position, you're guessing in the dark and they get to react to you. The exact same two cards win more money in position than out of position, every single time. And it's free — it depends only on your seat.

Quick way to lock it in: the button is always in position (it acts last against everyone). The blinds are always out of position after the flop (they act first). Everyone in between is in position against the seats to their right, and out of position against the seats to their left.

See it in a hand: it folds to you on the button, you raise, and the big blind calls. The flop comes. The big blind has to act first and checks — now you decide, already knowing what they did. That's playing in position.

Now flip it: you're in the big blind, the button raises, you call. The flop comes and you have to act first, with no idea what the button will do. Same flop, worse spot — that's out of position.

Early, middle, and late position

The seats also get grouped into buckets, and you'll hear these words constantly. They just describe how early or late you have to act:

Early position: the first seat to act — in 6-max that's UTG. You act before almost everyone, so you play the tightest range. The worst seats to be in.

Middle position: the bridge seat — in 6-max that's the HJ. Better than early, not as good as late.

Late position: the seats nearest the button — the CO and the BTN. You act last or nearly last, so you play the widest range. The best seats.

The blinds: the SB and BB — forced-bet seats that are stuck out of position after the flop, so they're their own special category.

The simple version: early = tight, late = wide, blinds = special. That grouping is the same at every table size — it's the framework worth memorizing, not the individual seat names.

More or fewer players

Tables come in other sizes. 9-handed is "full ring" — and live $1/$2 and $1/$3 in Vegas are almost always full ring. It just adds more early seats in front. Going toward the button at 9-handed you get UTG, UTG+1, UTG+2, then LJ, HJ, CO, BTN, plus the blinds. (LJ is "lojack" — just the name for one of those extra middle seats, poker slang riffing on "hijack." A 6-max table doesn't have one.) Fewer than six is "short-handed," which happens as players bust or leave.

One rule covers both directions: more players behind you = play tighter; fewer players = play wider. You don't need a separate chart per table size. Position = how many players still act after you. A 6-max UTG range is basically a full-ring middle-position range. Master 6-max first; full ring is just "bolt two tighter early seats on top."
